## Manuscript rewrite architecture — PO-lite hidden line — 2026-09-30 13:58 +08:00
- Decision: Partial Observability (PO) will be treated as a **hidden conceptual line**, not as a new explicit framework or claimed contribution in this Major Revision.
- Rationale: the manuscript currently lacks a clear explanation for why observation-readiness is needed. The underlying issue is that a biological pathway/process is only partially represented by the genes/features effectively observed and mapped in a given transcriptomic dataset. This PO logic repairs the missing rationale without expanding the revision into a new theoretical paper.
- Scope boundary: do NOT add `Partial Observability` to the title; do NOT introduce a named PO framework in the Abstract; do NOT create a new PO Methods section; do NOT claim that this paper establishes or validates PO theory.
- Introduction: add only a concise bridge that the biological pathway and its dataset-observed representation are not equivalent; pathway computability therefore does not automatically imply adequate empirical representation for interpretation.
- Methods: define the observation unit and define `K` before any category uses it. `K` is the effective matched-gene representation in the analyzed dataset. Only after this definition introduce observation-ready, low-resolution, and near-unobservable categories. Explain that these categories describe dataset-specific representation adequacy, not biological importance.
- Reader-order rule: no symbol, category, metric, or analysis should appear before its purpose and operational definition are introduced. In particular audit `K`, `D`, observation-ready, low-resolution, near-unobservable, size-matched random baseline, 95th percentile, coverage, GSVA, and ssGSEA.
- Scoring distinction: mean-z, GSVA, and ssGSEA answer how an adequately represented pathway is scored; they are not competitors to observation-readiness itself. Observation-readiness is upstream of scoring. No scoring method was uniformly superior, which should be interpreted as evidence that representation adequacy and scoring-method robustness are distinct analytical layers.
- Core evidence chain for rewrite: partial dataset representation (implicit PO rationale) → observation readiness → representation adequacy (`K`) → pathway scoring → task-specific discriminability (`D`) → matched-random calibration → robustness / external validation → evidence qualification → bounded endpoint-specific claim.
- Key logical constraints to make explicit: `K >= 10` does not imply `D >= 1.301`; nominal endpoint discriminability does not imply above-random pathway-specific evidence; internal association does not imply external transportability or clinical prognostic utility.
- Conclusion gate: preserve the idea that a computable pathway-level result is not yet a justified endpoint-specific conclusion. The accumulated evidence should determine the maximum defensible claim rather than allowing a direct jump from signal/pathway/p-value to biological or clinical conclusion.
- Claim-language preference: avoid claiming improved formal statistical `precision` unless measured. Safer concepts are `claim calibration`, `evidence qualification`, `bounded interpretation`, or `more precisely calibrated endpoint-specific interpretation`.
- Future lineage: this paper may serve as an early conceptual/empirical entry point for later explicit PO work, but that future connection remains implicit in the present revision.
- Rewrite strategy: retain reviewer-requested analyses but integrate each into the scientific argument. Every major analysis must answer an explicitly introduced scientific question before its method/result appears. Avoid the appearance of disconnected reviewer-driven additions.
- Working title candidate for later HUMAN GATE: `Observation-readiness assessment separates pathway representation adequacy from task-specific discriminability in cancer transcriptomics`. Do not finalize title until the semantic rewrite and claim-ceiling audit are complete.
